## Purpose

Explain what first principles thinking is, how to do it in two steps, which techniques reach a first principle, and where the method fails.

## When to Use / When NOT

Full excavation is not required for the method to pay off: going a level or two deeper than most people already gains a lot of value.
<!-- ease:claim id=a-level-or-two-deeper-has-value -->

It is not effortless. The two steps sound simple, and finding the first principles takes focused thinking.
<!-- ease:claim id=requires-focused-thinking -->

## Core Knowledge Content

### Definition

A first principle is a basic truth that cannot be broken down further.
<!-- ease:claim id=first-principle-is-irreducible-truth -->

The working move is to separate what is known to be true from what is an assumption.
<!-- ease:claim id=separate-truth-from-assumption -->

Farnam Street scopes the definition: the elements sought are those irreducible in the context of the given situation.
<!-- ease:claim id=irreducible-in-context -->

The search is not necessarily for absolute truths.
<!-- ease:claim id=not-necessarily-absolute-truths -->

Our understanding of first principles evolves as knowledge grows.
<!-- ease:claim id=understanding-of-first-principles-evolves -->

### The two steps

1. Break the problem down to its most basic truths. Both sources describe this decomposition.
<!-- ease:claim id=break-down-to-basic-truths -->

2. Rebuild a solution from those truths.
<!-- ease:claim id=rebuild-solution-from-principles -->

### Techniques for reaching a first principle

Farnam Street names two techniques for identifying first principles: Socratic questioning and the Five Whys.
<!-- ease:claim id=socratic-and-five-whys-are-two-techniques -->

**The Five Whys.** A researcher digs deeper by repeatedly asking "why".
<!-- ease:claim id=five-whys-repeats-why -->

Doing so uncovers the root causes of problems.
<!-- ease:claim id=five-whys-finds-root-causes -->

Five is not a hard limit: you do not have to stop there, though five is generally enough to reach a first principle.
<!-- ease:claim id=five-is-not-a-fixed-count -->

**Socratic questioning.** It can establish first principles through stringent analysis.
<!-- ease:claim id=socratic-questioning-establishes-first-principles -->

Untools counts six types of questions to ask when digging for the fundamental truth.
<!-- ease:claim id=socratic-six-question-types -->

### In practice

A Dropbox designer describes the loop on a real project: break the problem into its fundamental parts, address each part, then reconfigure them to build up a solution.
<!-- ease:claim id=dropbox-design-example -->

## Failure Modes (Mandatory)

Never practising the method: without learning to take things apart, test assumptions and reconstruct, we end up bound by what other people say is possible.
<!-- ease:claim id=untested-assumptions-bind-to-convention -->

Leaning on analogy: analogies limit beliefs about what is possible and let people argue without exposing faulty thinking.
<!-- ease:claim id=analogies-limit-what-seems-possible -->
